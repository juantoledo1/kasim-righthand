# Kasim's Frameworks

Kasim has developed **100+ business frameworks** (per `textWeb.md`). The frameworks below are the **selected set the sources actually describe** — they are not a complete list.

## 1. 3X Freedom Framework

**Freedom from Limitation → Freedom of Purpose → Freedom to Evolve**

Developed from 15+ years in entrepreneurship and coaching (per the authority hub). It is the organizing idea behind the free 3X Freedom community.

## 2. Scale vs. Non-Scale

**Scalable activities → AI can increasingly automate them. Non-scalable activities → human connection, differentiation, opportunity.**

- Kasim describes scale as finding the "paved road" from A to B; once found, you're "off 100 miles an hour."
- His core claim: **"Anything that scales, AI can do."** In a post-AI world, all paved roads are traversable by AI — so entrepreneurs should deliberately look for things that **do not** scale.
- Practical structure: **find something that doesn't scale + has the potential to significantly impact the business.**
- Examples he gives: direct mail, personal outreach, events, speaking, and relationship-building.

## 3. Give First → Give Last → Give More

**Give first → Give last → Give more than expected**

A relationship-and-reciprocity framework:

- Be the first one to give — show up giving.
- Give more than anybody would expect.
- Always be the last one to give.
- This evokes the law of reciprocity "in a way that will blow your mind and change your life."

## 4. Dream 100 Framework

**Identify 100 high-value relationships/clients → consistently pursue them → look beyond the obvious customer → identify people or organizations that can introduce you to many more opportunities.**

- Your Dream 100 is "the list of 100 clients that you wish you could have."
- It should be so robust and powerful that **even obtaining one or two could change the face of your business** — "if the dream 100 isn't robust enough, then you're not thinking big enough."
- He expands the idea: **customer → organization → organization that controls/accesses many customers.** Example (from the interview): instead of one septic customer (worth one pump), target an HOA with a shared lift station (300 homes = 300 contracts), or a property-management company that *feeds you* customers.
- Kasim notes he did not invent it and does not remember who he learned it from.

## 5. Problem → Underlying Need → New Solution (thinking pattern)

A reasoning pattern rather than a formally named framework:

- Instead of asking "How do I replace LinkedIn outreach?", ask **"What problem was LinkedIn outreach actually solving?"**
- Underlying need: people want to connect through shared interests.
- New question: what **non-scalable** method can create that connection?
- Possible solutions: industry communities, events, groups, masterminds, forums.

This pattern captures **how he thinks**, not just his vocabulary — useful grounding for AI-assisted work on his material.
